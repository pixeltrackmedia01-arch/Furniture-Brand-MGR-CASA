# Standing Desk vs Sitting Desk: Which to Buy in India

Source: https://featherlitestore.com/pages/standing-desk-vs-sitting-desk-india

The post-COVID office furniture boom in India sold a lot of standing desks to people who did not need one. This is the honest guide: who benefits, what to spend, and how to use one without wrecking your knees in three months.

## Who actually benefits from a standing desk

The research has narrowed in the last few years. Standing desks reliably help if you:

* Work 8+ hours a day at a desk and have lower back pain that worsens by afternoon
* Have a sedentary job (programmer, designer, writer) and add no other movement during the day
* Are tall (over 5'11") and your current desk is below 70cm: sitting at a too-low desk causes shoulder problems

Standing desks *do not* meaningfully help if you:

* Already get up and walk regularly during the day
* Sit for 4-6 hours total (most office workers)
* Have knee or hip problems that standing aggravates

## Sitting desk + good chair vs Standing desk

For most Indian office workers, the upgrade order is: **better chair first, standing desk second.** A great office chair fixes most ergonomic problems for less money. A standing desk is the answer when you have already optimised your chair and your back still hurts.

That said, if your chair is already decent and you sit 8+ hours, a standing desk is the next meaningful upgrade.

## Motorized vs pneumatic: what's the difference?

### Pneumatic (gas-spring)

A lever releases a gas piston that lets you raise or lower the desk by lifting it. **Pros:** half the price of motorized, no electricity needed, silent. **Cons:** limited weight capacity (usually 15-25 kg of stuff on top), harder to raise if you have heavy monitors, the gas spring degrades over 3-5 years.

### Motorized (electric)

Press a button, the desk raises or lowers itself. **Pros:** handles heavy desk setups (40+ kg), preset memory positions for sit/stand heights, lasts longer. **Cons:** costs 1.5-2× pneumatic, needs a power outlet nearby, has a small whirring sound.

**Our pick for most people:** motorized if your desk setup is heavy (two monitors + laptop stand + speakers); pneumatic if it's just a laptop and a monitor.

## The mistake that wrecks knees: standing all day

The most common standing desk failure mode is "I bought it so I should use it." If you stand 8 hours straight, your knees, ankles, and lower back will hurt worse than they did sitting.

The healthy pattern, per the ergonomic research: **roughly 1 hour standing for every 2 hours sitting.** Start with 15 minutes per hour and work up over a month. Use a thick mat under your feet (₹2,000-4,000). Wear shoes or slippers, not bare feet.

## Pair it with the right chair

A standing desk does not eliminate the need for a good chair: you will still sit for half your day. If you are spending ₹40,000 on a desk, do not pair it with a ₹3,000 chair. Match the investment.

## The one accessory that matters

A monitor arm. Without one, when you stand up, your monitor stays at sitting-eye-level, and you end up looking down, which defeats half the ergonomic benefit. Spend the extra ₹4,000-6,000 on a gas-spring monitor arm and adjust it once per height change.

## If you only do one thing

If you are not sure whether you actually need a standing desk, do this for two weeks: set a timer to stand up every 30 minutes and walk around for 2 minutes. If your back pain reduces, you might benefit from a standing desk. If it doesn't change, the desk won't help you either, and your money is better spent on a better chair or a physio.
